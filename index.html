The story behind Snapcam

Why Alexander built it

Alexander kept losing the photos that mattered most. A Polaroid from a friend's wedding, a snapshot from a rooftop evening, a blurry-but-perfect picture of a night he never wanted to forget — one by one they slipped out of pockets, got tucked into books, or simply vanished. The moment stayed in his memory, but the little print was gone.

He loved the feeling of an instant photo: the warmth, the imperfection, the way a print you can actually hold turns a moment into something real. But he never liked the flat, over-polished look of standard phone or professional camera photos — they felt cold, endlessly scrolled and quickly forgotten.

So he made his own answer. Snapcam is an instant camera with the soul of a Polaroid and the memory of the internet: it's connected, so the second you press the shutter your photo is already shared and safely saved — before the print has even finished developing in your hand.

You get the best of both worlds. The physical keepsake to keep, and the digital copy that can never be lost.

— Alexander, founder of Snapcam
